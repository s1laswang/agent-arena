## Summary

### Company failure simulation: core concept
- Central demo idea: simulate a major tech company's collapse (e.g. Yahoo, Nokia) from peak valuation to zero.
  - Nokia case study: rejected the touchscreen on a consultant's advice while holding ~50% of the global phone market.
  - "Failure" defined broadly: bankruptcy, but also stagnation, loss of innovation, becoming a rent-seeking corporation.
- Grounded in real, documented company failures, not hypothetical scenarios.
  - Pull from research papers on human behaviour and organisational decision-making.
  - Feed the findings to the agents so they carry human bias patterns before they act.
- Self-driving car analogy for AI management: humans have 2 eyes, get tired, get emotional; an AI has more sensors and no fatigue.

### Mole agent and deception detection
- Core mechanic: a "mole" agent inside a simulated company, trained with RL to maximise deception.
  - Mole at mid level (not C-suite, not powerless) to balance capability and realism.
  - The mole exploits human bias in the other agents: speculation, grudges, miscommunication, emotional decisions.
  - The mole can recruit accomplices, climb the ranks through charm, extract data from higher-ups.
- Employee agents modelled on realistic human behaviour.
  - Biases: speculating about others' intentions without talking to them, conflict-driven decisions, poor cross-team communication.
  - "3-year-old test": flag decisions a child would see as irrational but adults make because of emotion or bias.
- RL training loop: pause the current cluster jobs, use the full GPU capacity to train the mole toward maximum deception.
  - Several difficulty levels to avoid collapse (the Gemini "I quit" failure mode is a known risk).
  - The reward must keep agents active, not passive.

### Junction challenge strategy
- Rubric priorities: the gap between frontier agent and reference (30), reward integrity (25), meaningful difficulty and explainable mistakes (30).
- Plan: optimise the mole/deception system for the challenge scoring first, then add broader bias detection with the remaining time.
- Demo: an engaging video of the simulation, ideally with an interactive part where viewers try to beat the AI.
  - The game must be finishable in about 10 minutes for the peer-review audience.
  - Top 5 projects from the peer-rated video round go on to pitch (best overall prize).
- The Polymath agent run is already done and can be submitted separately.

### Next steps

- Source research papers on human organisational behaviour and bias.
- Identify real company failure cases to anchor the simulation.
- Design the demo video and the interactive part for Junction peer review.
